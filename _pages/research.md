<p class="msa-eyebrow">RESEARCH</p>
<h1 class="msa-page-title">Human-centered inquiry into trust, safety, and digital life</h1>
<p class="msa-intro">My research investigates how people understand and navigate digital and AI-mediated environments, especially when the consequences of misunderstanding, misplaced trust, or exposure can be significant. I combine qualitative and mixed-methods inquiry with computational techniques to understand social contexts and design more accountable systems.</p>
<div class="msa-rule"></div>
<h2>Research directions</h2>
<div class="msa-stack">
<article class="msa-row-feature"><span class="msa-index">01</span><div><h3>Human-Centered and Trustworthy AI</h3><p>Understanding how people assess the reliability of AI-generated outputs, make decisions under uncertainty, and calibrate reliance on opaque systems. I am particularly interested in explanation, trust calibration, synthetic media, and the role of context in what counts as a meaningful explanation.</p><p><strong>Contexts:</strong> Information integrity, healthcare, and higher education.</p></div></article>
<article class="msa-row-feature"><span class="msa-index">02</span><div><h3>Usable Security and Privacy</h3><p>Examining how privacy, digital safety, and surveillance are experienced and negotiated in everyday life, including during periods of political uncertainty and crisis. This includes the practical limits of individual security practices when risks are social, institutional, and collective.</p><p><strong>Contexts:</strong> Sociopolitical movements, scams, and digitally vulnerable populations.</p></div></article>
<article class="msa-row-feature"><span class="msa-index">03</span><div><h3>Social Computing and Digital Well-Being</h3><p>Investigating mediated interaction, information sharing, help-seeking, and access to support. My work considers how technological interventions can account for local values, social relationships, resource constraints, and marginalization.</p><p><strong>Contexts:</strong> South Asian communities, student learning, and mental health support.</p></div></article>
</div>
<h2>Approach and methods</h2>
<p>I work across <strong>semi-structured interviews, focus groups, survey research, thematic and interpretive analysis, inferential statistics, and interpretable machine learning</strong>. I view methods as choices driven by a question and its social context, rather than as a fixed technical toolkit. I am particularly drawn to collaborations that connect empirical understanding with design, deployment, and evaluation.</p>
<div class="msa-note"><strong>Research positioning.</strong> My primary disciplinary home is HCI. AI/ML and quantitative analysis complement qualitative inquiry; they are not substitutes for understanding people and situated contexts.</div>
